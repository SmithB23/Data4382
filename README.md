# State SSVF Funding Analysis & Forecasting
2. Business Problem / MotivationEnsuring that veterans and their families have access to stable housing is a critical national priority. The Supportive Services for Veteran Families (SSVF) program provides vital funding to non-profits to prevent homelessness, yet the allocation of these resources across different states can be complex. This project aims to identify the underlying drivers of funding distribution—such as homelessness rates and VA medical expenditures—to provide a data-driven framework for anticipating future resource needs and ensuring equitable support for veterans.
3. Project OverviewThe goal of this project is to analyze and forecast SSVF award amounts from 2013 to 2025. By merging federal funding data with Point-in-Time (PIT) homelessness counts and VA expenditure reports, the project employs machine learning (XGBoost) and econometric models (GMM PVAR) to predict funding needs. Key results include a predictive accuracy ($R^2$) of 0.946 and the identification of medical care expenditures as a primary driver of funding.
4. DataSources:VA SSVF Reports: Award amounts and grantee data.  HUD Exchange PIT Counts: Veteran homelessness census data.  VA GDX Reports: Geographic distribution of VA expenditures.  Type: Multi-year panel data (Time-series across US States).  Size: Data spans 2011 to 2025 across all 50 US states and territories.  Key Features: Total SSVF Award, Sheltered/Unsheltered Veteran Counts, Medical Care Expenditures, and Veteran Population.
5. Data PreprocessingCleaning Steps: Automated extraction of tables from PDFs using pdfplumber and standardization of inconsistent column names across different fiscal years.  Handling Missing Values: Applied the Shapiro-Wilk test to assess data distribution; used Mean imputation for normal distributions and Median for non-normal distributions.  Feature Engineering: Generated lagged variables (e.g., $t-1$ homelessness counts) to enable predictive forecasting and used log transformations for econometric stability.
6. Exploratory Data Analysis (EDA)Insight 1: Strong correlation observed between state-level veteran population and total SSVF funding received.  Insight 2: Significant variance in the ratio of sheltered vs. unsheltered veterans across different regions, impacting the type of support services required.  
7. Modeling ApproachBaseline Model: Panel OLS with entity effects to establish a fundamental relationship between veteran population and funding.  Advanced Model 1 (Machine Learning): XGBoost (Lagged). Chosen for its ability to capture non-linear relationships and high-dimensional interactions.  Advanced Model 2 (Econometrics): GMM PVAR (Generalized Method of Moments Panel VAR). Chosen to handle endogeneity (e.g., funding affecting homelessness while homelessness affects funding) using instrumental variables.
8. Model TrainingTools Used: Python (scikit-learn, xgboost, rpy2) and R (panelvar package).  Hyperparameters: Optimized learning rates and tree depth for XGBoost; specified lag lengths for the PVAR model.  Training Process: The dataset was split by year to simulate a forecasting environment, using historical data to predict future award cycles.
9. ResultsMetrics:$R^2$ (Coefficient of Determination): Chosen to measure the proportion of variance in funding explained by the model.  RMSE (Root Mean Square Error): To quantify the average dollar-amount deviation in predictions.  Model Comparison:ModelR2 ScoreStandard XGBoost0.946  Lagged XGBoost0.926  Panel OLS(Baseline)
10. Model InterpretationSHAP Values: Used to provide "Global" and "Local" explanations for predictions.  Drivers: The model reveals that Medical Care expenditures and Total Veteran Population are the most significant predictors of SSVF awards.  Visualizations: SHAP waterfall plots illustrate how specific state metrics (like high unsheltered counts) increase the predicted funding amount.
11. Key InsightsWhat worked best: The Lagged XGBoost provided the best balance of predictive power and practical utility for forecasting.  Impact: This model allows policymakers to anticipate which states will require increased SSVF funding based on shifting homelessness trends and VA medical spending.
12. ConclusionThis project successfully demonstrates that SSVF funding is largely driven by identifiable veteran demographics and medical expenditures. By leveraging GMM-based econometric models, we can account for the complex feedback loops inherent in social service funding.
